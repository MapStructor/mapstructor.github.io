Privacy

Last updated 30 July 2026

The short version: MapStructor collects what it needs to run your account and your maps, and nothing else. There is no advertising, no tracking pixels, and your data is never sold or handed to data brokers.

What is collected

  • Your account — email address and login credentials, handled by Supabase Auth. Passwords are stored hashed; nobody at MapStructor can read them.
  • Your maps and data — everything you draw, upload, import or type into a map, plus any images you attach.
  • Your profile — whatever you choose to put on it. A profile is only public if you make it so.
  • Operational counts — how much storage you use, how many edits and imports happen per day, and whether an account was active in a month. These are numbers used to keep the service running and priced honestly, not a record of what your maps say.
  • Payment records — if you subscribe, Stripe holds your payment details. MapStructor stores only a customer reference and which plan you're on. Card numbers never touch this service.

What is not collected

No advertising or analytics trackers, no third-party cookies, no location tracking, no scraping of your map contents for any purpose other than serving them back to you.

Cookies and local storage

Your browser stores a login session so you stay signed in, plus small preferences like which map you last had open. That's it — no advertising cookies.

Who else touches your data

Running the service means using a few infrastructure providers. Each one only processes what it needs to:

  • Supabase — database, authentication and file storage.
  • Cloudflare — serving map tiles and files, and the website itself.
  • Backblaze B2 — encrypted off-site backups.
  • Stripe — payments, if you subscribe.
  • GitHub — hosting the site's code and running data-processing jobs for large imports.
  • Mapbox and open basemap providers — the background maps. Loading a map means your browser requests tiles from them.
  • An email provider — for account emails such as password resets.

Public versus private

New maps are private. A map becomes visible to others only when you publish it or share a link. If you make a map public, assume it can be seen, copied and indexed by search engines. Making it private again removes it from the site, but doesn't recall copies other people already made.

Backups

Encrypted backups run nightly and rotate out after 14 days. This means deleted data can persist in a backup for up to two weeks before it's gone for good.

Getting your data out, or deleting it

Every layer can be exported from the editor at any time, and a whole project can be downloaded as a standalone copy. To have your account and its data deleted, email contact@mapstructor.com and it will be done — subject to the backup window above. If you're in a place with a legal right of access, correction or erasure, that same address is how to exercise it.

Children

MapStructor isn't directed at children under 13, and accounts aren't knowingly created for them. If you believe a child has an account, write and it'll be removed.

Where data lives

Servers are in the United States. Using the service means your data is processed there.

Security

Connections are encrypted, backups are encrypted, and payment details never reach this service. No small service can promise perfect security; if a breach ever affects your data, you'll be told what happened and when.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, it'll be announced on the site before it takes effect, and the date at the top will change.
